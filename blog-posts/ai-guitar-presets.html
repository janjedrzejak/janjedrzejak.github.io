AI, a guitar and 58 tones: an experiment after hours

19 OCT 20264 MINJAN JĘDRZEJAK

After a day spent building automations, connecting systems and exploring AI, I sometimes want something entirely different: no dashboards, no meetings — just a guitar, an amplifier and a favorite song. That is where this experiment began.

Could an AI assistant turn a conversation about a song into a real settings file that I could import into my Yamaha THR10II? Not compose music or play guitar in my place — simply help me enjoy playing after work.

It started with Dire Straits

I shared an existing Yamaha THR10II .thrl6p preset and asked for the distinctive guitar tone from Money for Nothing. That meant going beyond advice such as 'increase the mids': the assistant had to understand the file structure, select amplifier characteristics and write a valid preset.

The next question was obvious: what if I prepared an entire library rather than a single sound? The result is 58 song-inspired presets for six artists — with separate clean and distorted variations where a song needs them.

58 PRESETS · 6 ARTISTS · YAMAHA THR10II

Nirvana (14), Jimi Hendrix (8), Megadeth (8), Metallica (12), Eric Clapton (8) and David Bowie (8).

A real Yamaha THR10II preset, not just a description

These are presets made specifically for the Yamaha THR10II amplifier, saved in its compatible .thrl6p preset format. They are meant to be imported into Yamaha THR Remote — not audio files, impulse responses or captures that you need to convert.

The library contains 58 .thrl6p files, organized by artist and, where useful, by clean and distorted parts of a song. After import, each setting is a starting point you can adjust using the amplifier and THR Remote.

From a prompt to a file you can actually use

What fascinates me is not only that AI knows something about guitar sounds. It is the ability to connect natural language with structured files, hardware settings and a concrete result. Gain, equalization, amplifier models and effects become part of a practical dialogue.

It is the same underlying idea I work with professionally: understand an intention, use the right data and tools, then create something usable. This time the outcome is not a business process or report, but a riff that sounds a little closer to the record.

Not TONEX or Quad Cortex — and that is the point

Let's be clear: a Yamaha THR10II is not an IK Multimedia TONEX or a Neural DSP Quad Cortex. This is not an exercise in capturing or profiling the exact behavior of famous rigs. Those are different tools with different capabilities.

But for building and exploring guitar tones, I think this is a brilliant little experiment. AI can suggest a useful starting point, package it in a format the amplifier understands and let me spend more time playing, listening and refining. That, to me, is the exciting part — not a claim of studio-perfect accuracy.

AI is not a substitute for ears — or practice

A preset is a starting point, not a faithful reconstruction of a studio recording. The guitar, pickups, volume, technique and room all affect the sound. Some effects — including a fixed-position wah — cannot be reproduced perfectly with the THR10II alone. Although the files were structurally checked, I have not tested every preset on a physical amplifier.

That is exactly why the experiment is fun. AI can reduce the tedious work between an idea and the first test. I still pick up the guitar, play, listen and tweak. The joy of making music remains human.

An evening of entertainment — and a glimpse of something bigger

We often discuss AI in the context of productivity, cost reduction and business transformation. Those applications matter, but they are not the whole story. When an assistant can work with real file formats and tools, creative and highly personal ideas become much easier to prototype.

I use AI at work to connect information and automate processes. This time I used a similar approach to make an evening with my guitar more interesting. Technology does not always have to solve a business problem. Sometimes it can simply expand what is possible — and make an ordinary evening more enjoyable.
